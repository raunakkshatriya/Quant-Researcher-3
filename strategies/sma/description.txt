SMA TREND FILTER - the baseline strategy
========================================
Run:  python step7_sma.py          (train period only; test period locked)
      python step7_sma.py --test   (one logged look at the test period)

WHAT AN SMA IS
SMA = simple moving average: the average of the last N closing prices, recalculated every day.
Closes 10, 11, 12, 13, 14 -> 5-day SMA = 12. A price of 14 is above its average (it has been rising);
a price of 9 would be below (it has been falling).

THE RULE
Hold a stock only while it is in an uptrend. When it is not, hold none of it and keep that share of the
money in cash (earning T-bills). Every stock is judged on its own.

WHAT WE TRADE (our specific execution): the same 49 stocks as the pairs strategy
(the 50 largest US companies on 2026-09-08, minus GOOG, which is the same company as GOOGL):
  NVDA AAPL GOOGL MSFT AMZN AVGO META TSLA MU LLY JPM WMT AMD V JNJ XOM MA INTC ORCL ABBV BAC CSCO PLTR CVX
  COST LRCX KO CAT MRK AMAT UNH GE MS PG DELL NFLX HD GS PM WFC PANW RTX GEV ANET KLAC AMGN TXN C TMO
Benchmark: equal-weight buy-and-hold of the same stocks (each stock that exists gets an equal share,
rebalanced daily, a simplification). The run also prints SPY for context.

THE PROCESS
1. SIGNAL     Each day, from closes up to and including today, compute the rule for each stock.
2. RULES      The lab tries five (train only):
                price>SMA50, price>SMA100, price>SMA150, price>SMA200   (price above its N-day average)
                SMA50>SMA200                                             (50-day average above 200-day: the
                                                                          classic "golden cross")
3. WEIGHTS    Money is split equally among all stocks that EXIST with enough history that day. A stock in an
              uptrend gets that equal share; a stock not in an uptrend gets nothing and its share stays in
              cash. So exposure moves between 0% and 100%. A stock needs N days of history before it can be held.
              Risk limit: max 25% in any one stock, max 100% total.
4. EXECUTION  Signal at the close of day t, trade at the close of day t+1, first return earned on day t+2.
5. COSTS      5 bp per $ traded (spread + slippage; commission is zero). Long-only, so no borrow fee.
              Whipsaw (flipping in and out around the average) means more trades and more cost; the run prints
              round trips per stock per year so you can see it.

WHY IT EXISTS
It is the simplest trend-following rule, so it is the bar every fancier strategy has to clear. The honest
question is not "is the SMA profitable?" (these stocks mostly went up) but "does timing beat just owning the
same stocks, or does it only smooth the ride?"

KNOWN WEAKNESSES
* Survivorship bias: the list is today's giants. Their past uptrends are known, so buy-and-hold looks great
  and the SMA mostly stays invested because the stocks mostly rose.
* A moving average always LAGS: it signals a turn only after the price has already moved. In sideways, choppy
  markets it whipsaws and pays costs.
* Typical outcome for timing rules: lower drawdowns with similar or lower return than holding, not higher profit.
* The 49 stocks are not 49 independent bets (many fall together), so many exits happen at once.
* Choosing among 5 rules is a small search; read the deflated Sharpe, but it cannot see survivorship.
* Related to the cross-asset trend strategy (step 4), which uses past returns instead of an average:
  this is a baseline, not a new source of return.
