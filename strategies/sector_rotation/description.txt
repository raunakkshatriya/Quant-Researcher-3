SECTOR ROTATION - what it does and how
======================================

THE IDEA
The stock market is made of sectors (technology, energy, health care, ...). Which sectors
lead the market changes slowly: a sector that has beaten the others over the last several
months tends to keep beating them for a while. This is called relative-strength or
industry momentum. The strategy simply holds the currently strongest sectors and changes
the list once a month.

WHAT WE TRADE (our specific execution)
Eleven "SPDR Select Sector" funds (ETFs). Each fund holds the S&P 500 companies of one sector:
  XLK  Technology                 XLF  Financials
  XLV  Health Care                XLE  Energy
  XLY  Consumer Discretionary     XLP  Consumer Staples
  XLI  Industrials                XLB  Materials
  XLU  Utilities                  XLRE Real Estate (exists from Oct 2015)
  XLC  Communication Services     (exists from Jun 2018)
Before those two funds exist they are simply left out of the ranking, so early years rotate
between 9 sectors and later years between 11.
Benchmark for comparison: SPY (the S&P 500 index fund), bought and held.

THE RULES
On the last trading day of every month:
  1. For each sector fund, compute its price return over the last L trading days.
  2. Rank the funds from best to worst.
  3. Buy the top K funds, equal amounts (each gets 1/K of the money). Hold nothing else.
  4. Do nothing until the next month-end.
Long only: no shorting, no borrowing, always fully invested.

THE SETTINGS L AND K
We try L in {63, 126, 189, 252} trading days (about 3, 6, 9, 12 months) and K in {2, 3, 4}.
That is 12 combinations. Each is scored on the TRAIN period only (start of data to
2025-12-31) using the Sharpe ratio (average return divided by the size of the wiggles; higher
is better). The best combination is frozen. Only then do we look at the TEST period
(2026-01-01 onwards). The script prints which combination won.

HOW THE MONEY IS CALCULATED
- Decision at the end of day t, trade, and the new holdings earn the return of day t+1
  (the engine's one-day shift - see engine.py).
- Cost: 3 basis points (0.03%) on every dollar bought or sold.
- Start: $100 on the first day the strategy holds something. Return is compounded daily.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR IN THE RESULTS
- Does the strategy beat SPY after costs, or just match it with extra effort?
- Is the TEST result in the same ballpark as the TRAIN result? A big gap suggests the settings
  were a lucky fit to the past.
- The test period is under one year: treat it as a sanity check, not proof.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS
- Most of what a long-only stock strategy earns is simply the market going up. Compare to SPY.
- Sectors are defined by the fund provider and were redefined in 2018 (XLC was created
  from parts of XLK and XLY), so history is not perfectly consistent.
- Between month-ends we assume the weights are quietly kept equal. In reality they would drift
  slightly; the effect is tiny for monthly strategies.
- ETF prices from Yahoo Finance are end-of-day snapshots, not guaranteed tradable prices.
