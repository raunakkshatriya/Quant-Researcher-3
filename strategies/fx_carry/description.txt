FX CARRY + MOMENTUM - what it does and how
==========================================
Run:  python step6_fx_carry.py          (train period only; test period locked)
      python step6_fx_carry.py --test   (one logged look at the test period)

THE IDEAS (two separate signals, tested alone and mixed)
CARRY: Holding a currency that pays a higher interest rate than the dollar earns you that interest gap
every day. Historically the high-rate currencies have, on average, NOT fallen enough to cancel the
interest (economists call this the "forward premium puzzle"), so buying high-yielders and shorting
low-yielders has paid on average. The risk is that it pays small amounts for a long time, then loses a lot
very fast when markets panic and everyone sells the high-yielders at once (2008, and the Swiss franc
shock of January 2015 for franc shorts). That crash risk is the price of the return, not a flaw to remove.
MOMENTUM: Currencies that have risen over the past 6-12 months tend to keep rising a little. This is weaker
than in stocks, and the two signals are not the same bet: carry buys high-rate currencies, momentum buys
recent winners, and these are often different currencies.

WHAT WE TRADE (our specific execution): 9 currencies against the US dollar
  EUR (EURUSD=X), GBP (GBPUSD=X), JPY (JPY=X, inverted), CHF (CHF=X, inverted), AUD (AUDUSD=X),
  NZD (NZDUSD=X), CAD (CAD=X, inverted), NOK (NOK=X, inverted), SEK (SEK=X, inverted).
  Prices: Yahoo Finance daily closes. "Inverted" = Yahoo quotes foreign-per-dollar; we flip it to
  dollars-per-foreign-unit so up always means "the foreign currency got stronger".
  Interest rates: FRED / OECD 3-month interbank rates, monthly (IR3TIB01{EZ,DE,GB,JP,CH,AU,NZ,CA,NO,SE,US}M156N).
  Benchmark: cash (T-bill). A currency book with long and short legs is not compared with SPY.

THE PROCESS
1. TOTAL-RETURN INDEX. For each currency, one day's return = exchange-rate change
   + (currency's interest rate - US interest rate) / 252. Holding the currency long earns that
   differential; being SHORT a high-rate currency PAYS it. The backtest engine then trades these indexes.
2. NO PEEKING AT RATES. The OECD value for a month is an average over that month and is published weeks
   later. We treat the value dated month m as known only from the start of month m+2. If a series stops
   updating, after 150 days we stop trusting it and that currency is left out of the ranking (the data
   report printed at the start shows each series' last observation).
3. MONTH-END RANKING among the eligible currencies (need an exchange rate, a known carry, and enough
   history; at least 6 eligible):
     carry rank     = rank of the interest differential vs USD
     momentum rank  = rank of the exchange-rate change over the last `lookback` days (126 or 252)
     combined       = w_carry * carry rank + (1 - w_carry) * momentum rank
4. POSITIONS. BUY the top 3 (1/6 of our money each), SHORT the bottom 3 (1/6 each). Gross exposure 1.0,
   long and short roughly cancel, so the whole-dollar move mostly cancels. Held until next month-end.
5. EXECUTION. Signal at the close of day t, trade at the close of day t+1 (engine delay).
6. COSTS. 3 bp per $ traded (majors cost about 1 bp; NOK, SEK, NZD more). No separate borrow fee: financing
   is already inside the interest differential. The collateral earns the T-bill rate.
7. SETTINGS THE LAB TRIES (train only): w_carry in {0, 0.5, 1} x lookback in {126, 252} = 6 combinations.
   (With w_carry = 1 the lookback is irrelevant, so those two rows are identical: carry-only.)
   Reading the ranked table tells you which ingredient works: carry only (1.0), momentum only (0.0), or the mix.

KNOWN WEAKNESSES
* Carry crash risk: long periods of small gains, short episodes of large losses. A Sharpe ratio understates this.
* Short history: Yahoo's EUR starts in Dec 2003, so there are only about 21 years of train data and a few
  carry crashes. One or two events decide the result.
* Monthly OECD interbank rates are a rough proxy for what a trader really earns (forward points, bank spreads).
  The 2-month delay is deliberately conservative. Real implementations use forwards or swap points.
* Yahoo FX closes are not a tradable quote and differ by source; they are fine for research, not execution.
* Only 9 developed-market currencies. Emerging-market carry is larger but much riskier and needs better data.
* Gross exposure 1.0 gives a low-volatility book (probably around 4-5%). Do not add leverage until an edge is proven.
