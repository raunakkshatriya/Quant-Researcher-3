NEWS SENTIMENT - step 10
========================
Run order:  python step10_news_sentiment.py fetch | score | ic | run   (details in the script's docstring)

THE IDEA
Headlines move stocks. A language model reads each headline about a company and says whether it sounds like good
or bad news. If stocks with recently positive news then do better than the others, we can hold those.

WHAT WE TRADE (our specific execution): the same 49 stocks as the SMA and pairs steps
(the 50 largest US companies on 2026-09-08, minus GOOG which is the same company as GOOGL):
  NVDA AAPL GOOGL MSFT AMZN AVGO META TSLA MU LLY JPM WMT AMD V JNJ XOM MA INTC ORCL ABBV BAC CSCO PLTR CVX
  COST LRCX KO CAT MRK AMAT UNH GE MS PG DELL NFLX HD GS PM WFC PANW RTX GEV ANET KLAC AMGN TXN C TMO
Benchmark: equal-weight buy-and-hold of the same stocks (each existing stock an equal share, rebalanced daily).

DATA
Alpaca News API (articles supplied by Benzinga), history from 2015, free with an Alpaca account. Only headlines are
used. Articles tagged with more than 3 tickers (market roundups) are ignored. The same headline for the same stock on
the same day counts once.

THE PROCESS
1. SCORE      FinBERT (ProsusAI/finbert, run on your GPU) gives each headline P(positive), P(negative), P(neutral).
              Score = P(positive) - P(negative), from -1 (bad) to +1 (good). Each article is scored once and cached.
2. TIMING     Decision at the 4pm New York close of day t; trade at the next close (the engine's 1-day delay). Only news
              published BEFORE 4pm NY on day t may be used on day t. News at or after 4pm, at weekends, or on holidays
              is first usable on the next trading day. Time zones are converted properly (summer/winter).
3. SIGNAL     For each stock: the average score of its articles over the last `window` trading days (1 or 5), needing at
              least 3 articles, otherwise no opinion.
4. WEIGHTS    On the last trading day of each week: hold the 10 stocks with the highest signal, 10% each (needs at
              least 15 stocks with an opinion that week). The rest is cash earning T-bills. Held until next week.
5. COSTS      5 bp per $ traded. No shorting.

THE TWO PHASES
Phase A ("ic"): before any backtest, measure whether the signal predicts next-day and 5-day relative returns at all
  (rank correlation = information coefficient, plus top-fifth minus bottom-fifth return). No signal = stop.
Phase B ("run"): the usual lab experiment (2 settings: window 1 or 5), deflated Sharpe, walk-forward, versus the
  equal-weight benchmark, crisis windows (reporting only). Test period (2026-) stays locked unless --test.

KNOWN FLAWS (read these)
- News is usually priced in within minutes; we trade at the next DAILY close. Any edge is probably small.
- These are mega-cap stocks everyone reads: the hardest place to find a news edge.
- About 10 years of data: Sharpe estimates are noisy (standard error about 0.3).
- FinBERT was trained on formal sentences, not headlines; many headlines come out neutral or misjudged.
- Survivorship: the stock list is today's biggest companies.
- Coverage of the news feed can change over time; "fetch" prints articles per year so you can see it.
- Half-days (1pm close) are not modelled (about 3 days a year).
