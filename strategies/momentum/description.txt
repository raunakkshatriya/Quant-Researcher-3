CROSS-SECTIONAL MOMENTUM - what it does and how
===============================================

THE IDEA
Stocks that have done well over the past 6-12 months tend to keep doing better than other
stocks over the next month; stocks that did badly tend to keep lagging. Researchers have seen
this in many markets since the 1990s (Jegadeesh & Titman, 1993). A common explanation is that
news and fundamentals sink into prices gradually rather than instantly. "Cross-sectional"
means we compare stocks AGAINST EACH OTHER on the same date (winners vs losers), not each
stock against its own past.

Benchmark for comparison: SPY (S&P 500 index fund), bought and held.

THE RULES
On the last trading day of every month:
  1. For each stock compute its return from L trading days ago up to 21 trading days ago.
     (We skip the latest month because very recent winners tend to bounce back a bit.)
  2. Rank the stocks from best to worst.
  3. mode "long":        buy the top K stocks, equal amounts.
     mode "long_short":  buy the top K (half of our money) and SHORT the bottom K (the other
                         half). Shorting = borrow shares, sell them, buy them back later.
                         If the market falls, longs and shorts both fall, so they cancel.
  4. Hold until the next month-end.

THE SETTINGS L, K AND MODE
L in {126, 189, 252} days (6, 9, 12 months); K in {5, 10}; mode in {long, long_short} = 12
combinations. Each is scored on the TRAIN period only (start of data to 2025-12-31) by Sharpe
ratio; the best is frozen, then evaluated on the TEST period (2026-01-01 onwards).

HOW THE MONEY IS CALCULATED
- Signal uses the close of day t. We trade at the close of day t+1 (one-day delay) and the new
  holdings first earn day t+2's return.
- Trading cost: 5 basis points (0.05%) per dollar bought or sold. This is one number covering
  commission (zero at Alpaca), bid-ask spread, and slippage on large US stocks. No separate
  market-impact model (zero at $100). A cost-sensitivity table (0, 3, 10, 25 bp) is printed.
- Short borrow fee: 0.25% a year on the amount shorted (placeholder for easy-to-borrow
  large caps; hard-to-borrow stocks cost far more).
- $100 start, compounded daily, starting on the first day the strategy holds something.

CASH
Any money not invested earns the 3-month T-bill rate (FRED DTB3); Sharpe ratios are on excess
returns over that rate. A dollar-neutral long/short book is assumed to earn the rate on its
collateral, which is a simplification.

KNOWN LIMITATIONS (read these)
- SURVIVORSHIP BIAS: the list is today's winners. In 1995 we are "choosing" companies that we
  already know became giants. This inflates train results, especially for long-only. The
  honest fix needs historical index membership data, which is not free. Use the TEST period
  and the settings comparison as the more reliable signal.
- Large-cap momentum is a weaker effect than in the full stock market; with only 50 names,
  ranks are noisy.
- Momentum can suffer sharp crashes when the market turns (e.g. 2009), mostly in long_short.
- The test period is under one year; its Sharpe ratio is noisy.
- Shorting needs a margin account and borrow availability; a $100 paper account may not allow it.
